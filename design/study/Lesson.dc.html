In 30 seconds

Enzymes speed reactions until every active site is busy. Km tells you how much substrate it takes to reach half speed. Inhibitors slow things down in two different ways, and they leave different fingerprints on the graph.

01

Michaelis–Menten

Reaction speed rises with substrate concentration, then levels off at Vmax, when every enzyme is busy. Km is the concentration that gives half of Vmax. A low Km means the enzyme grabs its substrate easily.

Quick check · 10 seconds
What does a low Km tell you about an enzyme?
02

Inhibition

A competitive inhibitor sits in the active site, so more substrate can push it out. A non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere and lowers Vmax however much substrate you add.
